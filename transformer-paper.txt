Attention Is All You Need

Ashish Vaswani, Noam Shazeer, Niki Parmar, Jakob Uszkoreit, Llion Jones,
Aidan N. Gomez, Lukasz Kaiser, and Illia Polosukhin. NeurIPS 2017.
arXiv:1706.03762. This compact source extract is built from the official paper
and keeps the claims, equations, measurements, and figure descriptions used by
the interactive reading map.

1. PROBLEM AND MOTIVATION
The paper describes recurrent neural networks as sequence models whose
computation is inherently sequential. Aligning positions to computation steps
precludes parallelization within training examples. The paper states: "The
fundamental constraint of sequential computation, however, remains."

Attention mechanisms can model dependencies without regard to their distance,
but earlier systems commonly combine attention with a recurrent network. The
paper proposes a model that relies entirely on attention to draw global
dependencies between input and output.

2. CENTRAL PROPOSAL
The proposed Transformer is "based solely on attention mechanisms, dispensing
with recurrence and convolutions entirely." It uses an encoder-decoder
structure and is designed to allow significantly more parallelization.

3. ENCODER AND DECODER
The encoder is composed of a stack of N = 6 identical layers. Each encoder
layer has a multi-head self-attention sub-layer and a position-wise
feed-forward network, with residual connections and layer normalization.
The decoder is also composed of a stack of N = 6 identical layers. It adds a
third sub-layer for attention over the encoder output and masks future tokens.
All sub-layers and embedding layers produce dmodel = 512 dimensional outputs.

4. SCALED DOT-PRODUCT ATTENTION
An attention function maps a query and a set of key-value pairs to an output.
The paper says: "The output is computed as a weighted sum of the values."
For scaled dot-product attention, query-key dot products are divided by the
square root of dk and passed through softmax. The matrix expression is
Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QK^T / sqrt(dk))V.

5. MULTI-HEAD ATTENTION
The model linearly projects queries, keys, and values h = 8 times and performs
attention in parallel. Each head uses dk = dv = dmodel/h = 64. The heads are
concatenated and projected. The paper explains that multi-head attention can
jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces at
different positions.

The Transformer uses attention in three ways: encoder-decoder attention,
encoder self-attention, and masked decoder self-attention. The mask prevents
leftward information flow that would violate autoregressive generation.

6. POSITION-WISE FEED-FORWARD NETWORK
Each encoder and decoder layer contains a feed-forward network applied to each
position separately and identically. It uses two linear transformations with a
ReLU activation. The inner-layer dimensionality is dff = 2048 while input and
output use dmodel = 512.

7. POSITIONAL ENCODING
Because the model contains no recurrence or convolution, positional encodings
are added to token embeddings. The paper uses sine and cosine functions and
says: "The wavelengths form a geometric progression from 2π to 10000 · 2π."
The authors also experiment with learned positional embeddings and report nearly
identical results, choosing sinusoids because they may extrapolate to longer
sequences.

8. COMPUTATIONAL TRADE-OFF
The paper compares self-attention, recurrent, convolutional, and restricted
self-attention layers. For self-attention, the table reports O(n^2 · d)
per-layer operations, O(1) sequential operations, and O(1) maximum path length.
Recurrent layers require O(n · d^2) operations, O(n) sequential operations,
and O(n) maximum path length. Restricted self-attention has O(r · n · d)
operations and O(n/r) maximum path length.

9. TRANSLATION EXPERIMENTS
The experiments use WMT 2014 English-to-German and English-to-French machine
translation. The best Transformer reaches 28.4 BLEU on English-to-German and
improves over existing best results, including ensembles, by over 2 BLEU.
On English-to-French, the model reaches 41.8 BLEU after training for 3.5 days
on 8 GPUs. The paper says a new state of the art can be reached after as little
as 12 hours on 8 P100 GPUs.

10. GENERALIZATION TO PARSING
The paper evaluates English constituency parsing. A 4-layer Transformer gets
91.3 F1 in the WSJ-only setting and 92.7 F1 in the semi-supervised setting.
The table also reports 93.0 F1 for a multi-task baseline and 93.3 F1 for a
generative baseline, so the parsing result demonstrates generalization but is
not the strongest number in every comparison.

11. QUALITATIVE ATTENTION EVIDENCE
The attention visualization follows a long-distance dependency in encoder
self-attention layer 5 of 6. The caption says: "Many of the attention heads
attend to a distant dependency of the verb ‘making’, completing the phrase
‘making...more difficult’." Different colors represent different heads.

12. LIMITS AND NEXT STEPS
The authors plan to investigate local, restricted attention for very long
sequences and to extend the Transformer to images, audio, and video. They also
identify making generation less sequential as a future research goal.

13. CONCLUSION
The paper presents the Transformer as the first sequence transduction model
relying entirely on self-attention without sequence-aligned RNNs or convolution.
It reports new state-of-the-art results on both WMT 2014 translation tasks and
makes training more parallelizable. The conclusion describes the model as
"attention, replacing recurrent layers with multi-headed self-attention". The training and evaluation code is
identified as tensor2tensor.
