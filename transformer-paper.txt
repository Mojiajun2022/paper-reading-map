Attention Is All You Need

Ashish Vaswani, Noam Shazeer, Niki Parmar, Jakob Uszkoreit, Llion Jones,
Aidan N. Gomez, Lukasz Kaiser, and Illia Polosukhin. NeurIPS 2017.
arXiv:1706.03762. This file is a compact, source-linked reading extract from
the official paper PDF, retaining the claims and measurements used in the demo.

ABSTRACT AND INTRODUCTION
The paper describes recurrent and convolutional sequence models as difficult to
parallelize because computation is sequential. The paper states: "The fundamental
constraint of sequential computation, however, remains."

The proposed Transformer is "based solely on attention mechanisms, dispensing
with recurrence and convolutions entirely." It relies entirely on attention to
draw global dependencies and is designed to allow more parallelization.

MODEL ARCHITECTURE
The Transformer uses an encoder-decoder structure. The encoder is a stack of
N = 6 identical layers, and the decoder is also a stack of N = 6 identical
layers. The model uses representation dimension dmodel = 512. Each encoder
layer has multi-head self-attention and a position-wise feed-forward network;
the decoder adds encoder-decoder attention and masks future positions.

ATTENTION MECHANISM
The paper defines scaled dot-product attention using queries, keys, and values.
It says: "The output is computed as a weighted sum of the values."
The dot products are divided by the square root of dk before softmax. Multi-head
attention uses h = 8 parallel attention layers, with dk = dv = dmodel/h = 64.
The paper explains that multi-head attention can attend to information from
different representation subspaces at different positions.

POSITION AND COMPUTATION
Because the model has no recurrence or convolution, positional encodings are
added to token embeddings. The paper uses sine and cosine functions and says:
"The wavelengths form a geometric progression from 2π to 10000 · 2π."
For self-attention, the table reports O(n^2 · d) per-layer operations, O(1)
sequential operations, and O(1) maximum path length. Recurrent layers require
O(n) sequential operations and O(n) maximum path length.

TRANSLATION RESULTS
On WMT 2014 English-to-German, the best Transformer reaches 28.4 BLEU and
improves over the existing best results, including ensembles, by over 2 BLEU.
On WMT 2014 English-to-French, the paper reports 41.8 BLEU after training for
3.5 days on 8 GPUs. The paper says the model can reach a new state of the art
after being trained for as little as 12 hours on 8 P100 GPUs.

GENERALIZATION AND LIMITS
For English constituency parsing, the 4-layer Transformer reaches 91.3 F1 in
the WSJ-only setting and 92.7 F1 in the semi-supervised setting. A multi-task
baseline reaches 93.0 F1 and a generative baseline reaches 93.3 F1 in the table.
The paper evaluates translation and parsing; it does not claim that every
sequence task will benefit equally, and it notes future work on local attention
for large inputs and outputs.

CONCLUSION
The paper presents the Transformer as a sequence transduction model based
entirely on attention, replacing recurrent layers with multi-headed self-attention.
It reports new state-of-the-art results on both WMT 2014 translation tasks and
makes the training process more parallelizable. The code used for training and
evaluation is identified as tensor2tensor.
